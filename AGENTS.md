# AGENTS

Project-specific rules. The shared global instructions still apply.

## Rendering engine

- Renders must be deterministic per absolute time. Any randomness added to
  the engine or native nodes must derive from absolute time or parameters
  (see `globalThis.__renderOffset`), never from `Math.random` call order.
  Chunk seams are verified sample-for-sample and fail otherwise.
- Do not use superdough features that still run as AudioWorklets (pulse,
  coarse, djf, ladder filter, env/bmod modulators) unless you add a native
  replacement in `src/engine/native-nodes.js`. Render with worklets disabled
  so such features fail loudly.
- Patches to superdough live in `scripts/build-strudel.js` with exact anchors.
  When upgrading Strudel, rebuild with `node scripts/build-strudel.js`; a
  missing anchor means a patch must be re-checked.

## Composition

- The target is the four reference mixes in
  `docs/music/reference-analysis.md`. Read it before changing the sound. Measure
  with `tools/reference-analysis`, which treats renders and references alike.
- After adding or changing an instrument preset or drum kit, run
  `npm run calibrate` and commit `src/compose/calibration.json`.
- The kit is generated by `scripts/build-kit.js` into the ignored `assets/kit`
  at install. Keep it deterministic, since scores depend on it.
- Every style's intro and outro must stay beatless: the stream crossfades
  between any two tempos.
- "Noisy" in this project means a full middle, not hiss. The references have
  more top end and roughness than our old library. What they have and it lacked
  is space: a 250-1600 Hz floor of -29 to -50 dB, loudness swing of 3.7-6.6 dB,
  and hats near -22 dB against the kick. Protect those. Never fix noisiness by
  darkening the mix.
- Keep one chord part and one melody at a time. Voice chords explicitly with
  `voiceLoop` (rootless, four or five notes, no neighbouring semitones), not
  Strudel's `voicing()`. Harmony moves slowly: house loops change at most every
  two bars. The filter arc and block lines supply movement instead.
- A held note under the sidechain is not a rolling bass. House low end is one
  held sub; a plucked line, when a song has one, is two or three notes a bar and
  stays off the kick's steps.
- Hooks are short, repeated phrases crossing a bar line, not running lines.
- Keep each orbit's reverb settings constant within a track (`builder.js`
  buses). Changing room size per event regenerates the impulse response. Rooms
  stay short and dark, because long halls fill the gaps.
- Held chords use the tied patterns (`heldA`), so the sidechain pumps one note
  instead of re-striking it every bar.
- Superdough duck gain is `1 - sqrt(depth)`; set depths in dB with `b.duck`.
- Check sound changes on the main body of rendered tracks (`file@60+120`), since
  intros and outros drop the kick by design. Compare per family with `cmp.py`.
  Reference ranges: 90% of energy below 400 Hz, centroid 250-350 Hz, crest near
  11.5 dB for house; 76% and about -24 dB above 2 kHz for slow.
- Spectral matching gets the mix right, not the music. Read the score and the
  sheets for the musical decisions, and the user decides what sounds good.

## Stream

- Backpressure in the encoder comes from the video pipe only; waiting on the
  audio pipe deadlocks while x264 fills its lookahead.
- A fatal encoder output error must close both input pipes, so FFmpeg can
  finish joining its reader threads. Propagate its actual exit code before
  marking a programme complete.
- The scene (`src/stream/scene`) draws on one 640x360 pixel grid. The look is
  fine dither, matching `assets/scene/reference.png`, not chunky pixels. Output
  is a whole-number enlargement (`--scale 3` is 1080p, 2 is 720p). Never scale a
  sprite at runtime: mixed pixel sizes read as a knockoff. A smaller object gets
  its own art, or a larger block in the sheet table.
- All colour goes through one light model in `palette.js`. Day and night keep
  the same depth order, sky lightest and near land darkest.
- The land is painted, not coded. Long plates in `assets/scene/plates` (hills),
  `near` (meadow, trees) and `front` (bottom plants), with flat magenta for
  anything see-through, go through `node scripts/build-plates.js` into
  `plates.bin`, with one palette per family so depth is graded in `palette.js`
  (`PLATE_LOOK`). One set per season, same layout. Joins bend both plates to a
  shared skyline, then cut along the path where they differ least and drop anything
  left stranded; never dissolve in noise, which leaves tree tops hanging in the sky.
  Procedural trees and hills never reached the
  reference; ask an image model for a continuation of the reference instead.
- Characters come from grid sheets (flat magenta, white cell lines) through
  `node scripts/build-sprites.js` into `sprites.json`. Its `SHEETS` table sets
  each sheet's block size, which fixes the sprite's size on screen. Both scripts
  need ffmpeg; commit their output.
- Houses are rare but present: villages live inside plates 6 and 7 and a farm in
  near plate 4, so one passes every few minutes rather than never or constantly.
- The fox moves right at least 95% of the time. Never add a stop that breaks
  this: stops are the workout (every fifth leg, under 80 s, with controlled reps:
  pull-ups at the bar, dips at the rails ahead of it, back for muscle-ups, on past
  the rails for push-ups) and a 5-second telescope look. Travel is walking,
  bicycle, rowing, sailing and broom. Friends
  pass without stopping. The only text on screen is the two top chips.
- Country, season and events are laid out by distance in `journey.js`; sky and
  weather come from the wall clock in `clock.js`. A season is a place, so the
  fox walks into winter and distant layers show it first. Landscape chunks must
  stay pure functions of position, with no per-frame or call-order randomness.
- The live sender runs on the VPS. The NAS is storage only. Prepare H.264/AAC
  programmes on a workstation with `src/cli/program.js`, which plans once and then
  draws chunks in parallel from scene snapshots (`plan.js`, `chunk.js`); `src/stream/relay.js`
  sends them with stream copy and a bounded current-plus-ten-chunk cache.
  Deployment and encrypted credentials are owned by
  `~/.config/home/modules/services/openpost-radio/default.nix`. The module
  packages a reviewed copy of `src/stream/relay.js` as its sibling `relay.js`;
  sync that artifact and its source revision when changing the live sender.
  A programme
  repeats until replaced; do not describe this as fresh live generation.
  Create or replace the YouTube broadcast before restarting the relay: a
  broadcast that attaches to an hours-old sender session makes YouTube's
  ingest loop its 8-second prebuffer instead of advancing.
  Run `scripts/prewarm-relay-cache.sh` before a programme-adopting restart: a
  cold cache leaves a multi-minute ingest gap, and YouTube answers any gap by
  looping its buffered window on air.
  Publish audio before the catalog with `scripts/publish-nas-library.sh`.
- The relay must shift PCR, PTS and DTS together onto one continuous transport
  clock before feeding repeated programme chunks to FFmpeg. Raw timestamp resets
  at the eight-hour wrap provoke FFmpeg's 33-bit wrap correction. Keep the
  repeated-wrap regression and verify served YouTube audio against the source;
  encoder liveness alone does not prove clean playback.
- A timestamp discontinuity at a programme wrap gave FFmpeg different offsets for
  audio and video, 26.5 hours apart. It is the likely cause of the broken sound of
  2026-11-01, not a proven one. Treat any `timestamp discontinuity` line in the relay
  journal as an incident. `memory.events` counts file cache filling the limit too, so
  its `max` line alone is not evidence of memory pressure; check `oom_kill`.
- Prime the PCM input before waiting on video at encoder startup. FFmpeg 7
  can otherwise deadlock on Linux socket buffers during stream discovery.
  Drain waits must remove their close handlers; racing a pending lifetime
  promise per video frame retains handlers for the entire broadcast.
- The Converge mark appears only in the brand chip. The sound meter lives in the
  now-playing chip, under the title, and draws from the same `bands` as the audio. `logo.js` rasterises one
  quadrant on whole pixels and mirrors it so the gaps stay open.
- The fox is painted frame by frame, in everything: walking, pedalling, rowing,
  sailing, the broom and the workouts. Never replace a painted cycle with computed
  limbs or a still body: it was tried, and the fox lost all its life. Only bicycle
  wheels and the bar are drawn in code, because they must not flicker. Image models
  never redraw a face the same way twice, so `HEADS` in the build script pastes one
  master head into every frame of a cycle.
- All sheets show the fox at one size. A sheet's block size in `SHEETS` is what sets
  it, so compare a new sheet against `walk12` beside it, by the head.
- Walking is the painted `walk12` poses, laid out by `walkCycle` in the build script:
  each pose is placed by where its planted heel sits, and `walkFrame` steps through
  them with the ground, so that foot stays on the road. Each pose keeps its own
  painted body, tail and bag, which is the walk's life; only the face is shared
  (`HEADS`). Freezing the body to one pose made the fox look dead, recolouring legs
  to fake the other leg leading left stripes and fully dark legs, and a fully
  repainted cycle (`walk8`) lost the fox's face. All three were rejected. The raincoat
  walks on the plain fox's poses with its hood and cape laid over them (`DRESSED`),
  because its own painted poses walked worse.
- `build-sprites.js` flattens painted fur (`flatten`): mottling lands somewhere else on
  every frame and reads as flicker. Keying must not touch reds: the broom scarf once
  vanished because the magenta test matched it.
- What a vehicle looks like empty comes from the frames it is ridden in (`moored`,
  `stillParts`), and the rails are measured once then lifted out of the dip frames
  (`railsOf`), so climbing aboard changes no pixel of them. Getting on or off is one
  short hop (`hop` in `index.js`). Hats change in mid-air inside a puff; the workout
  and the boats are bare-headed. Never draw a mount frame with its own vehicle or fox
  in it: that is how the doubled bike and doubled fox happened.
- The now-playing chip is worked out in `chip.js` from the frame number and the list
  of titles, so any worker can draw any frame. The title layer travels in extra rows
  under each raw frame and is split off in ffmpeg, so it cannot drift from the picture.
- The road is level on purpose. Only the backgrounds roll; a tilted road needs tilted vehicles.
- The fox stays horizontally centred.
- Several outputs share one encode through tee with a fifo per output; keep
  `-flags +global_header` or flv outputs lose their codec headers.

## Verification

`npm test`, then a short render (`npm run compose -- --style <s> --seed 1 --render tmp/x.wav`)
and a stream preview (`npm run stream -- --output tmp/p.mp4 --duration 60`) for
changes in those areas. For scene changes, look at stills across places, seasons
and weather (`npm run scene -- --at <iso> --place lake --season winter --weather snow`);
`npm run stream -- --tour` renders the scripted tour in `src/stream/tour.js`
(extend it when adding scene features).
