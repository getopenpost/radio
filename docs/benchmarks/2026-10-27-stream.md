# Stream measurements, 27 October 2026

The deployed sender runs on the four-core VPS. The NAS stores music and
pre-encoded programmes only. A workstation draws the scene, mixes the music,
and encodes H.264/AAC. The sender copies encoded packets to YouTube over RTMPS.

## Forwarding comparison

Three 60-second, real-time FFmpeg runs per format. CPU is `(user + system) /
wall time`, expressed as a percentage of one logical core. The table reports
medians; ranges show the three individual CPU results. RAM is the median of
FFmpeg's maximum RSS. These runs copy packets to the null muxer, so they
exclude the Node wrapper, NAS fetching, FLV muxing, and the YouTube connection.

| Format | CPU, one core | Observed range | FFmpeg RAM |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 720p24 | 1.24% | 1.16–1.29% | 36.1 MiB |
| 720p30 | 1.01% | 0.92–1.04% | 36.5 MiB |
| 1080p60 | 1.68% | 1.63–1.78% | 39.3 MiB |

The small 24/30 difference is not evidence that increasing frame rate reduces
CPU. Different encoded packets and short samples affect these low figures.
Both cost about one percent of a core in this isolated comparison.

The 1080p60 sample was generated at 60fps, with 7,200 video frames over 120
seconds, rather than doubling a 30fps movie. Its measured bitrate was
3.454 Mbps under a 6 Mbps video budget. It took 31.8 seconds to prepare on the
laptop while another programme was being generated. An eight-hour programme
would take roughly two hours at that observed speed; scenes and load vary.

## Complete live sender, 720p30

A 355.7-second cgroup sample includes the Node sender, FFmpeg, and NAS reads,
including a refill across a four-minute chunk boundary:

- Mean CPU: **7.94% of one core**, equivalent to **1.98% of four-core capacity**.
- Median five-second CPU window: 6.88% of one core. Highest window: 42.90%
  during buffer work, below the configured 50% quota.
- Mean cgroup memory: **110.8 MiB**, including filesystem cache.
- Sum of process RSS: 104.0–124.7 MiB. Shared mappings may be counted twice.
- Memory limit: **128 MiB**. No OOM events, OOM kills, or automatic restarts.
- Output: **3.157 Mbps**, about 34.1 GB per day if sustained.
- Cache: approximately 1 GiB, current chunk plus ten ahead, about 40 minutes
  of future material. The whole music library is never downloaded to the VPS.

CPU excludes other VPS services and the shared Tailscale process. The sample
is a short sustained measurement, not a claim of multi-day endurance.

YouTube Studio reported LIVE and Excellent health. The public watch page
played 1280x720 video with advancing time and decoded audio bytes. The
1080p60 figures above are a forwarding benchmark and preview, not a claim
that the public event has switched to that format.

## Deployment and operating bounds

The Nix module is `~/.config/home/modules/services/openpost-radio/default.nix`.
It packages its reviewed `relay.js` in the Nix store, supplies SOPS credentials,
and owns the systemd unit, resource limits, state, cache, and restart policy.
The runtime source is music commit `d561a87`; its Nix deployment was confirmed
through the rebuild wizard at configuration commit `bf1f3d7`. Later commit
`13025bd` removes the retired NAS runtime and obsolete credential, without
changing the active VPS unit.

The library has 96 tracks, totaling 25,787 seconds, approximately 7h 10m.
The first prepared broadcast is eight hours and repeats. Fresh tracks and
seasonal changes require preparing a replacement programme. World days last
40 minutes, with 1–4 activities and half the schedule reserved for walking.

At a 6 Mbps video budget, 1080p60 would need about 2.1 GB of bounded VPS cache
and roughly 66.5 GB/day of upload including 160 kbps audio, before overhead.
Its full RTMPS sender cost has not been measured in production yet.

All 24 existing project checks passed. Syntax and the deployed runtime were
checked directly. Nix formatting and statix passed, and the VPS system built
and activated successfully. The fleet-wide no-build flake check remained
blocked by an existing invalid `cabal2nix-cachix.drv` in the Darwin evaluation.
